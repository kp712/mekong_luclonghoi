# Switch the weather and rain, keep the rest

Two of the app's eight inputs should change, and the station tests in this research show why. **NASA POWER should give way to ERA5 (through Open-Meteo) for temperature and ET0, and NASA GPM IMERG should give way to CHIRPS v3.0 for rain.** POWER's daily maximum runs 1.5–3.2 °C too hot on average in Feb–Apr at three Delta stations (up to 3.9 °C in March), so it counts 2.4 to 24 times too many days of 35 °C or more, and it overstates dry-season ET0 by about 1 mm/day in March and April. IMERG Final stopped at September 2025, its replacement has slipped past its promised date, and it reads 23% wetter than the Cà Mau rain gauge (56% wetter in Dec–Apr). Both replacements are free, need no login, and make the pipeline simpler. The other inputs mostly stay. Salinity, soil and greenness have no outright replacement, but each can gain a complement. The El Niño index should move from ONI to NOAA's new official RONI, a one-file change that takes the 2019–20 season out of the El Niño list. The switches change farmer-facing numbers: heat days drop sharply, dry-season canal-water need falls, rain totals fall by about 3% at the Sóc Trăng pins, and some card scores move. The owner has to settle a few questions first: the averaging start year, a cold-snap rule that no gridded source can trigger, and how to treat the 2019–20 season.

## The verdict for each dataset

The criteria, in order, were accuracy validated in the Mekong Delta, freshness, finer resolution and a simpler pipeline. Any free licence was acceptable, and a longer consistent record was a plus. The table gives each verdict; the sections below give the evidence.

| Dataset (current source) | Verdict | Use instead or alongside | Deciding evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain (GPM IMERG V07 Final) | **Switch** | CHIRPS v3.0 monthly, 0.05°, for both the averages and the latest 12 months; v2.0 only as a whole-series fallback | IMERG Final ends Sep 2025; it reads +23% at the Cà Mau gauge against +13% for CHIRPS v3; CHIRPS needs no login and is current to Oct 2026 |
| Temperature, humidity, wind, sun, ET0 (NASA POWER) | **Switch** | Open-Meteo `models=era5` for Tmax, Tmin and FAO-56 ET0 | Best monthly mean temperature at all 3 stations; POWER's heat-day count is 2.4–24× too high; the ET0 gap comes from POWER's inputs |
| Greenness, crop calendar (MODIS NDVI, HLS) | **Keep MODIS, add HLS-VI** | HLS-VI v2.0 NDVI and NDWI (30 m) from LP DAAC STAC for recent years and the monthly chart | MODIS is the only record back to 2000; HLS-VI fixes resolution and freshness; Sentinel-1 is most accurate but is a new data type |
| Salinity (8-station paper + KTTV) | **Keep, add sources** | Cần Thơ provincial weekly XMAN bulletins (if found); the KTTV 10-day forecast as a new layer | No source beats measured station values; the national series missed the Feb 2026 peak |
| Soil (SoilGrids 2.0) | **Keep for clay only** | Ask SIAEP for the Delta WRB soil map; stop scoring pH | SoilGrids rates acid-sulfate sites as *less* acid than alluvial soil |
| El Niño index (NOAA ONI) and outlook (CPC text) | **Switch index; keep outlook** | `RONI.ascii.txt`; parse the CPC Alert System status line | RONI has been NOAA's official index since 1 Feb 2026; no machine-readable probability feed exists |
| Province outlines (2020, 63 provinces) | **Keep, add** | HDX COD-AB v02 (34 new provinces), or dissolve the old outlines | New provinces are whole unions of old ones |
| Profits and prices (published figures) | **Keep, add a price indicator** | FAO FPMA Bạc Liêu wholesale rice price (free JSON, monthly) | No survey-based cost-and-return series exists |

## ERA5 beats POWER on heat, temperature and ET0

The weather test compared NASA POWER, ERA5, ERA5-Land and CHIRTS-ERA5 with daily GSOD records at Cần Thơ, Cà Mau and Rạch Giá from 2011 to August 2025 (Cần Thơ from 2014). Only days that every source had were compared ([GSOD](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/global-summary-of-the-day/access/); [POWER API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/daily/point); [Open-Meteo archive](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api); [CHIRTS-ERA5](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/experimental/CHIRTS-ERA5/)). POWER's problem is a seasonal swing rather than a constant offset. **POWER's Tmax is too high by +1.5 to +3.2 °C in Feb–Apr and too low by 0.9 to 1.9 °C in Jun–Nov.** Its daily spread is far too wide: the standard deviation of Tmax is 3.22 °C at Cần Thơ against 1.94 °C at the station, and POWER's highest Tmax there is 43.7 °C against the station's 37.0 °C. Because the swing cancels over the year, POWER's annual bias looks harmless (−0.04 °C at Cần Thơ). That is why the problem was easy to miss. POWER's Tmax correlates with the station at only 0.52–0.63; the ERA5 family reaches 0.64–0.86.

The swing distorts the two numbers the app takes from temperature. The first is heat days. The app counts days of 35 °C or more while crops are in the field, and that count feeds the "Weather & salt" card and the pest-pressure weight in `build.py`. The second is monthly mean temperature, which drives the rice pest rule.

| Heat days ≥35 °C per year | Station | POWER | ERA5 | ERA5-Land | CHIRTS-ERA5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cần Thơ | 29.8 | 71.4 | 11.2 | 10.8 | 11.2 |
| Cà Mau | 7.6 | 21.9 | 9.2 | 7.5 | 2.1 |
| Rạch Giá | 2.4 | 57.2 | 11.4 | 4.8 | 5.4 |

On heat days, ERA5-Land comes closest overall (mean absolute log-ratio 0.57, against 0.91 for ERA5 and 1.70 for POWER). All three ERA5-based sources undercount Cần Thơ by the same factor. That points to the station, an airport at the edge of the city, running hot, not to a source error; the app's pins are rice fields and resemble the grid cells more. For monthly mean temperature, **ERA5 at 0.25° wins at every station, with an RMSE of 0.34–0.44 °C and no seasonal swing**. POWER's RMSE is 0.77–1.21 °C. ERA5-Land runs about 1 °C cold from May to December, and CHIRTS-ERA5 runs 1–1.7 °C warm at two stations. That matches its method, which rescales ERA5 to a CHIRTS climatology and derives Tmin from that ([CHIRTS-ERA5 readme](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/experimental/CHIRTS-ERA5/aaa.readme.txt)). Published work supports ranking ERA5 first. An AFD study of four reanalyses against Vietnamese stations found that ERA5 did best ([AFD RP 396](https://www.afd.fr/en/resources/evaluating-performance-selected-reanalysis-datasets-over-vietnam)). A 174-site study found NASA's GEOS5 family, which includes MERRA-2 (POWER's source), less accurate than ERA5 for ET0 inputs ([Jewitt et al. 2026](https://edepot.wur.nl/709304)).

ET0 shows the same pattern. The app's own `et0_penman` on POWER inputs gives **about 9.5% more ET0 per year than Open-Meteo's ERA5 FAO-56 ET0 (+134 to +138 mm) at all three stations**. Almost all of the gap falls in Jan–May, peaking at +0.7 to +1.0 mm/day in March and April. Fed ERA5 inputs, the same `et0_penman` lands within 2–4% of Open-Meteo's ET0, so the formula is fine and the inputs cause the gap. In March, POWER's air is drier (64% against 74% relative humidity at Cần Thơ), its wind is stronger (about twice ERA5's at Cà Mau) and its Tmax is 3 °C hotter, while its sunshine matches ERA5's ([Open-Meteo S3 ERA5](https://openmeteo.s3.amazonaws.com/data/copernicus_era5/static/meta.json)). No station measures radiation, so the ET0 ranking rests on consistency rather than direct truth. Three checks agree with ERA5. ERA5's 3.2–5.2 mm/day range is close to the 3.5–5.0 mm/day monthly Penman–Monteith means published for Mekong stations, while POWER peaks at 6.1–6.2 ([J. Climate Change](https://content.iospress.com/articles/journal-of-climate-change/jcc220016)). A temperature-only Hargreaves estimate from the stations also sides with ERA5 on the dry-season peak. ERA5-Land cannot be used for ET0: Open-Meteo returned null ET0 on all 5,805 days requested.

Switching costs nothing in freshness. POWER's radiation, and so its ET0, lags 6 days, exactly as ERA5 does. POWER's temperature is 3 days fresher, which does not matter for monthly averages. Open-Meteo needs no key for non-commercial use and its data are CC BY 4.0. It caps each IP at 10,000 calls a day ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)), and the shared cloud IP hit that cap during testing. The same ERA5 series can be read without a quota from Open-Meteo's public S3 copy. **The recommendation is one source: Open-Meteo `models=era5`** for daily Tmax, Tmin and ET0. Avoid `best_match` and `era5_seamless`, which mix models and create breaks ([Open-Meteo docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)). ERA5-Land would win only if heat days mattered more than monthly means. CHIRTS-ERA5 should be skipped: it is warm-biased, too smooth and needs 7 GB yearly files.

The switch moves the app's numbers in three ways. Heat days at the station points fall from about 71 to 11 a year at Cần Thơ, 22 to 9 at Cà Mau and 57 to 11 at Rạch Giá, so the heat part of the "Weather & salt" card and the pest weight fall with them. The rice pest rule loses about 1–2 °C of false dry-season warmth and gains 0.5–1.2 °C in the wet season, which moves the pest peak toward the wet season. Dry-season ET0 falls by roughly 80 mm over Dec–Apr at the station points (my sum of the monthly gaps), which cuts the canal-water need in the salty months by about 15–20%. None of this has been rebuilt at the five Sóc Trăng pins: no GSOD station in Sóc Trăng or Tây Ninh has recent data (Sóc Trăng's stopped in 1997). Coastal pins need care because ERA5 changes sharply from one cell to the next at the coast. At Rạch Giá the land cell gives 163 heat days in the test period and the mostly-sea cell next to it gives 1.

The cold-snap test settled nothing. **No gridded source, POWER included, produced a single night with a 4.2 °C drop in Tmin (the shrimp cold-snap rule) at any station in 11–15 years.** Most station "cold snaps" are artefacts of sparse reports, such as a "previous night" minimum of 27–32 °C on days with only 4–8 observations. The one plausible event, Cần Thơ on 24–25 December 2017 (24.0 to 19.0 °C), appears as a drop of only 2.1–3.8 °C in the gridded sources. Changing the source will not make the rule fire. The rule itself has to change, which is an open decision below.

## CHIRPS v3 replaces a stalled IMERG and sits closer to the gauge

IMERG's main weakness is freshness. NASA's GPM team ended the V07 Final record at September 2025, when its input products moved to V08. The August 2026 notice moved V08 Final to "more likely" fall 2026, and no release had been found by 22 November 2026 ([GPM V08 update, Aug 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/update-imerg-v08-transition-schedule-aug-2026)). Even after release, V08 will be a new version: the app would have to refetch the whole record from 1998, and Final runs about 3.5 months behind ([GPM](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/imerg-v07-final-run-retrospective-processing-completed)). CHIRPS v3.0 runs from January 1981 to October 2026; the October file was posted on 13 November. It is 0.05° against IMERG's 0.1°, CHC has waived its rights, and it needs only plain HTTPS reads of cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs ([CHIRPS v3 COGs](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/monthly/global/cogs/); [README](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/README-CHIRPSv3.0.txt)). A test fetcher read all 550 months at the app's 19 points with `tifffile` alone, about 100 lines and no Earthdata login. That replaces the OPeNDAP fallbacks and 4-month batches in `fetch_gpm.py`.

At the app's 19 points, CHIRPS and IMERG agree closely on timing: **pooled monthly r = 0.93 for v3**, every point between 0.89 and 0.96, and r = 0.89 for dry-season totals. They differ in level. Over the app period (Dec 2000 – Nov 2024), v3 is 6% drier than IMERG for the year and 13% drier for Dec–Apr. Agreement is closest at the five Sóc Trăng pins, from −6% to +1% for the year. The largest gaps are inland, at An Giang (−23%) and Đồng Tháp (−20%) ([CHIRPS](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/)).

The gauge check answers which product is right. Of the three GSOD stations, only Cà Mau has a usable rain record: 255 complete months, 103 of them in Dec–Apr. Cần Thơ's file has no rain reports at all. **At Cà Mau, IMERG reads 23% above the gauge for the year and 56% above in Dec–Apr**, with Jan–Mar rain two to four times the gauge. CHIRPS v3 reads +13% (Dec–Apr +20%) and has the best monthly correlation (0.95, against 0.92 for IMERG) ([GSOD readme](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/global-summary-of-the-day/doc/readme.txt)). In 2018 and 2019, IMERG gave 3,000 and 3,460 mm against gauge totals of 1,931 and 2,192 mm. The test has a limit: CHIRPS ingests the Cà Mau gauge, so it is not independent for CHIRPS ([CHC station table, Jun 2018](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/diagnostics/monthly_station_data/2018.06.csv)). v3's correction for gauge undercatch also means it should sit above a raw gauge, so +13% is not plainly an error. Published work points the same way. Against 481 Vietnamese gauges, CHIRPS had about +4% annual bias in the Mekong Delta region, against +16% for CMORPH and −21% for GSMaP ([Sci Rep 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8752618/)). It also correlated best "in the Vietnam delta" within the Lower Mekong Basin ([Dandridge et al. 2019](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/rs11222709)). IMERG ranks first in upland Vietnamese basins on daily correlation ([Tran et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240007009/downloads/Robustness%20of%20SPPs%20for%20VN%20basins_manuscript_final.pdf)), but no study tests either product against Delta gauges at monthly scale. **No independent gauge exists in Sóc Trăng**, so absolute accuracy at the pins is unproven for every product.

The other candidates fail on one criterion or another. ERA5 rain is model output that rains too often over Vietnam ([Roversi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16050805)). MSWEP is non-commercial and needs an application form. GSMaP needs JAXA registration and shows −21% bias in the Delta. VnGP and APHRODITE are not current. CHIRPS v2 is 6% drier than v3 here, and the v3/v2 ratio shifts over time (1.13 before 2000, 1.06 after), so the two must never be spliced. v2 should serve only as a whole-series fallback.

At the five pins, the switch moves the **typical year from 2,071 to 2,007 mm and Dec–Apr rain from 172 to 155 mm**, with July and September lower and August higher. Taken alone, the drier dry season raises irrigation need slightly. Combined with the ERA5 ET0 cut of roughly 80 mm over Dec–Apr, the net canal-water figure still falls. The El Niño message survives: in CHIRPS v3, El Niño dry seasons get 86 mm against 209 mm in other years (−59%), close to IMERG's −52%. The "latest 12 months" view becomes current again: Nov 2025 – Oct 2026 totals 1,691 mm at the pins, a period IMERG Final does not cover. One fetcher detail needs fixing first. Four pins (Trần Đề, Mỹ Xuyên, Long Phú, Ngã Năm) sit exactly on a 0.05° cell edge, where the two neighbouring cells can differ by 9% in a month. The fetcher needs one fixed rule for edge points, or the pins need a small nudge off the edge.

## Greenness, salinity and soil keep their sources but each gains a complement

**For greenness, MODIS stays because nothing else covers 2000–2015**, the years before the coastal shift from double rice to rice–shrimp. Its validated Delta accuracy is modest: 80.6–85.5% for classifying cropping systems, and weakest for "spatially scattered" coastal rice, exactly the Sóc Trăng setting ([Chen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6010135)). With gaps between Delta seasons sometimes under 15 days, a 16-day composite can merge two crops into one peak ([Zhao et al., ESSD 2026](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/5583/2026/)). The most accurate method is Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through wet-season cloud: 93.84% mean overall accuracy over 2016–2024 for the whole Delta. It also found a steady loss of double-cropping in Sóc Trăng, Bạc Liêu and Trà Vinh ([Nguyen-Thi et al. 2026](https://www.recentadvancesin.com/remote-sensing/3020-6448-rars26001/)). But radar is a new data type with a new access route, and the Sentinel-1 constellation has been thin since 1B failed in 2021, with 1A retired on 29 June 2026 ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Time_to_say_goodbye_to_Sentinel-1A)). The cheaper complement is **NASA HLS-VI v2.0: ready-made 30 m NDVI and NDWI from Landsat and Sentinel-2, as cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs over CMR-STAC, about 2–3 days behind the satellite** ([LP DAAC HLSS30_VI](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/hlss30_viv002/); [HLS data](https://hls.gsfc.nasa.gov/hls-data)). The app's HLS data ending in November 2024 reflects how the pipeline fetches through AppEEARS; HLS itself is current. HLS-VI's NDWI also separates dry fallow from a flooded pond, which NDVI alone cannot do in the Feb–Apr "bare" test. Sources disagree on whether the HLS-VI back-processing to 2013/2015 is finished. Two open maps can check each pin's crop count once: Open-SEA-Rice-10 (10 m, 2020–21, Vietnam accuracy 0.981) and the ESSD 2026 30 m map (1995–2024 epochs, 83.7–87.6% accuracy). Their dataset licences still need checking on Zenodo ([Open-SEA-Rice-10](https://zenodo.org/records/14627003); [ESSD map](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21349862)). Skip VIIRS, which stays at 250–500 m and has had 2026 processing gaps ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/viirs-data-gaps-processing-delays-from-november-5-2026)). Skip WorldCereal too: it has no rice layer ([ESSD 15:5491](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/5491/2023/)).

**For salinity, nothing beats measured station values, but the national bulletins have a gap.** The 2025–26 national 10-day series began with bulletin XMAN-01 on 10 March 2026 ([KTTV](https://kttv.gov.vn/upload/thuyvan1/2026/3/10/dbqg_xman_20260310_1500.pdf)). The season's largest intrusion came in the 14–19 February spring tide ([DWRM report 91](https://dwrm.mae.gov.vn/noidung/Lists/ListTinTuc/Attachments/16455/260422%20-%2091%20BC%20TNN%20KBNN%20CUU%20LONG%20BC%20DINH%20KY%20T3.pdf)). The app's 2026 national values can therefore understate the season peak. The fetcher did not miss anything; the February bulletins do not exist. The best lead for filling that gap is the provincial hydro-met stations' weekly bulletins. Vĩnh Long's XMAN-17 of 7 May 2026 lists measured peak salinity at about 20 stations, with distance from the mouth, the day of the peak and the same period last year. Its issue number implies weekly issues from about January ([Vĩnh Long XMAN-17](https://thanhduc.vinhlong.gov.vn/Portals/1216/VILO_XMAN_20260507_1700.pdf)). A Cần Thơ city equivalent, which now covers former Sóc Trăng, would give weekly readings at Trần Đề, Long Phú and Đại Ngãi, including the February peak. It was not found online and needs a search from outside the sandbox or a request to the station. Two open CC BY 4.0 records add older seasons for three province points: Trà Vinh and Cầu Quan for 1990–2016 ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/fm996rrfyz/1)) and Sơn Đốc for 1996–2016 ([GFZ](https://dataservices.gfz.de/10.5880/gfz.4.4.2024.004/surface-water-salinity-measurements-at-son)). Neither covers Sóc Trăng. Rynan's IoT network, the SIWRP 2015 data and the MRC API all need permission, and model products can be shown only as model values, never as readings.

**For soil, SoilGrids should keep only its clay values.** No published study validates SoilGrids 2.0 in Vietnam. Point checks put topsoil pH at 6.4–6.5 at Tân Phước and 6.0 at Mộc Hóa, both in the Plain of Reeds, a major acid-sulfate area. Alluvial Đồng Tháp gets 5.6, so the map ranks the acid-sulfate sites as less acid ([SoilGrids REST](https://rest.isric.org/soilgrids/v2.0/properties/query); [Husson, AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/64745baf542a3f9f03b4dc35)). Clay is 37–42% at every point checked, so it carries little difference between pins. HWSD v2.0 is coarser (1 km polygons) and names no Vietnamese update ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/soils-portal/data-hub/soil-maps-and-databases/harmonized-world-soil-database-v20/en/)). FAO's GSNmap salinity layer reads 8–11 dS/m everywhere, even in the Đà Lạt highlands. The real upgrade is the Vietnamese regional soil map with WRB classes, including Thionic Fluvisols. SIAEP supplies it to researchers on request ([CTU Journal](https://ctujsvn.ctu.edu.vn/index.php/ctujsvn/article/download/6843/4885/24679)). Its licence is unknown.

## RONI, new outlines and a price series are small file swaps

**NOAA's Climate Prediction Center made the Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI) its official ENSO index on 1 February 2026** ([NWS PNS 26-05](https://weather.gov/media/notification/pdf_2026/pns26-05_Relative_ONI.pdf)). RONI subtracts the tropical-mean sea-surface temperature anomaly from Niño-3.4 to remove the warming trend that makes ONI's 30-year base drift ([NWS RONI fact sheet](https://www.weather.gov/media/climateservices/RONI.pdf)). The file `RONI.ascii.txt` is a drop-in replacement with three columns instead of four. Its latest values are revised for up to two months, so a rebuild can change the newest month ([CPC RONI](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/)). Under the app's rule (Dec–Feb at least 0.5), **only one season from 1997 to 2026 changes: Dec 2019 – Feb 2020 drops from El Niño (ONI 0.71) to typical (RONI 0.14).** The 2004–05 and 2014–15 seasons stay El Niño but only just (RONI 0.54). In the app's 2016–2026 salinity window, the 2019–20 season moves from the El Niño group to the typical one. For rain, the El Niño sample over the averaging period drops from 9 seasons to 8. ONI is still published, so nothing breaks if the switch waits.

For the outlook, **no free machine-readable ENSO probability feed exists**. CPC and IRI publish probabilities as images, the IRI Data Library now needs a sign-in, and Australia's BoM ended its ENSO outlooks in December 2024 ([BoM](https://media.bom.gov.au/releases/1246/improving-how-climate-information-is-communicated-to-the-community)). The CPC discussion text stays the source, but the parser must pick the right sentence. The November 2026 discussion reads "El Niño Advisory", and its "greater than a 79% chance" refers to a *strong-to-very-strong* event, not to El Niño at all ([CPC discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml)). A parser that takes the first percentage it finds reports the wrong event. The status line is the robust field.

For the outlines, the 2020 file of 63 provinces (geoBoundaries, CC BY 3.0 IGO) stays, because former provinces are the app's scoring unit ([geoBoundaries API](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbHumanitarian/VNM/ADM1/)). **OCHA's HDX COD-AB v02 is the official source for the 34 new provinces.** It comes from Vietnam's General Statistics Office, has Vietnamese names and P-codes, was published 25 September 2025 and is licensed CC BY-IGO ([HDX](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ab-vnm)). Because the 2025 merger joined whole provinces, dissolving the old outlines gives the same shapes. New Cần Thơ city measures 6,360.82 km² in HDX, against 6,396 km² for the three simplified old outlines combined. Dissolving keeps a single source; HDX adds official codes. GADM 4.1 is still pre-merger and restricted to non-commercial use.

For profits, **no free survey-based cost-and-return table exists for Delta crops by season**, so the published-figure medians stay. The useful addition is a price indicator. FAO's FPMA serves wholesale rice prices in VND/kg for Bạc Liêu, An Giang and Đồng Tháp, weekly and monthly through 12 November 2026, as free JSON sourced from the Vietnam Food Association. The Bạc Liêu 5%-broken monthly price was 12,000 đ/kg in November 2026 ([FPMA API](https://fpma.fao.org/giews/v4/global/price_module/api/v1/FpmaSerieDomestic/?iso3_country_code=VNM&format=json)). None of these series is a paddy or farm-gate price. VFA's weekly table gives a national paddy average (lúa thường 6,879 đ/kg, 5–12 Nov 2026) as HTML only ([VFA](https://vietfood.org.vn/thi-truong/gia-noi-dia/)). The World Bank Pink Sheet adds export rice and world fertiliser prices ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/research/commodity-markets)). The safest use is a source-named line, "rice price this month is X% above or below the 2020–26 median", rather than rewriting profits. Each 1,000 đ/kg change in paddy price moves profit by about 6–7 million đ/ha per crop at the 6.2–7.4 t/ha yields reported ([CTU J. Sci. 2025](https://ctujsvn.ctu.edu.vn/index.php/ctujsvn/article/download/7025/5153/33034)). The same survey of 60 rice households in Long Mỹ (31.0 million đ/ha) could join `profits.json` as a university figure. Shrimp, mung bean, watermelon and maize have no usable price series.

## Three new layers earn a place on the farmer's screen

The most useful new layer costs almost nothing: **the KTTV 10-day salinity forecast is already inside the PDFs the fetcher downloads**. Each dry-season bulletin forecasts the next 10 days; the 31 May 2026 bulletin closed the season with a level-1 salinity disaster-risk warning ([KTTV 31 May 2026](https://kttv.gov.vn//upload/thuyvan1/2026/5/31/dbqg_xman_20260531_1500.pdf)). Parsing that section and showing it as "KTTV forecast, issued <date>" gives a farmer an action ("fill the pond with fresh water now") in the months that matter. With a strong El Niño under way, Feb–Apr 2027 is when this card will matter most.

The second layer is the official planting calendar (lịch thời vụ), entered by hand twice a year with its source and issue date. The Đông Xuân 2026–27 guidance for the Delta sets early sowing on 10–30 October 2026 in coastal areas at risk of late drought, a second wave on 1–31 December, and a hard end on 10 January 2027 ([Cục Trồng trọt guidance](https://storage-vnportal.vnpt.vn/cmu-khdn/5342/Vanban/2026.6.19-cv-chi-dao-sx-trong-trot-ung-pho-elnino-2026_-_nqmanh_19062026180253__19062026180725_signed.pdf)). Reports disagree on how large the early wave is. Since July 2025 the issuer for former Sóc Trăng is Cần Thơ city's agriculture department. The third layer is an ECMWF SEAS5 seasonal anomaly from Open-Meteo's free seasonal API (36 km, monthly update) ([Open-Meteo seasonal](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/seasonal-forecast-api)). Its skill over mainland Southeast Asia is limited to about the first month and to ENSO years ([HESS preprint](https://hess.copernicus.org/preprints/hess-2023-56/hess-2023-56.pdf)), and the output is not bias-corrected. It should appear only as "wetter, drier or normal" before Hè Thu sowing, never as millimetres.

The remaining candidates were rejected. Upstream Mekong flow duplicates the salinity forecast and has no public API. SPEI duplicates the app's own rain-minus-ET0 and misses the real coastal drought, which is salt in the canals. GloFAS is a river model that fits poorly in a tidal delta. Subsidence matters on a decade scale, not a season. No current open map of rice-to-shrimp conversion was found. Sóc Trăng's light-trap planthopper counts would be valuable but are not published as data, so the app should quote the province's sowing window for dodging planthoppers each season rather than ingest raw counts.

## Six decisions belong to the app owner

**The averaging start year** comes first. The recommendation is to keep Dec 2000 – Nov 2024 (`build.PERIOD`) for both weather and rain. Both ERA5 and CHIRPS v3 can support a 1981 start, which would add six El Niño seasons (1982, 1986, 1987, 1991, 1994 and 1997, by starting year) to the nine the app has now. But Dec 1981 – Nov 2000 is 18% drier in Dec–Apr in CHIRPS v3 (133 against 163 mm), and the number of Vietnamese stations in CHIRPS fell about sixfold after 2007 ([CHC station count](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/diagnostics/stations-perMonth-byCountry/Vietnam.095.station.count.CHIRPS-v3.0.png)). An earlier start would therefore shift the typical year for reasons partly unrelated to climate. Rain and weather must move together or not at all, because the water balance and the El Niño split pair them.

**The cold-snap rule** comes second. At a 4.2 °C night-to-night drop, it never fires in POWER, ERA5, ERA5-Land or CHIRTS-ERA5. The choices are a lower threshold suited to 0.1–0.25° data, a drop over two or three days, or removing the rule. Whichever is chosen, the text should call it a model estimate. Third, **under RONI the 2019–20 season becomes typical**. That moves one season between the typical and El Niño salt figures and the rain split. The research did not confirm the common description of 2019–20 as a record salt year, so a rebuild should show the effect before the switch ships. Fourth, **ERA5 or ERA5-Land for heat days**: one source (ERA5) is simpler and best on means; ERA5-Land counts heat days slightly better but has no ET0 and runs cold in the wet season. Fifth, **soil pH** can stay with a "model" label or leave the score until the SIAEP map arrives. Sixth, **profits** can stay fixed with a separate price line, or move with price through a labelled model that needs a milling ratio no source supplied. Two technical choices remain that do not need the owner: a fixed rule for pins on CHIRPS cell edges, and a manual check of the ERA5 cell at coastal pins.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the app's errors were thought to come from. The doubts concerned salinity and soil, where the data are thin. The largest farmer-facing errors were in weather, where the data looked solid: a NASA product with a near-zero annual bias hid a dry-season warm swing that multiplied the heat-day count and inflated water need in exactly the salty months. Rain had a similar hidden wet bias in the dry season. Both fixes also simplify the pipeline: CHIRPS removes the Earthdata login and most of the retry logic, and ERA5 arrives with ET0 already computed. Accuracy, freshness and simplicity rarely all point the same way in a review like this.

The thin datasets will not be fixed by a better global product. Salinity, soil type, planting calendars and planthopper windows all exist in Vietnamese agencies' bulletins and maps, and none is published as open data. The next improvements to the app depend on getting access to the Cần Thơ salinity bulletins, the SIAEP soil map and the provincial calendars, not on more searching of global datasets. Every replacement here is still a model estimate at a nearby grid cell, and the app should keep labelling each number with its source.
