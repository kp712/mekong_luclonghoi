**How to read the sowing window.** The duration runs from sowing or transplanting into the field through the last harvest, excluding nursery time. Starting on the first of each month, the pipeline counts whole months using their actual lengths and a 28-day February until they cover the longest checked duration. For example, a January 1 start occupies January and February for 59 days and reaches March for 60. The app shows one continuous run of starts whose occupied months avoid the pin’s estimated salt months. A missing duration gives “no checked growing time”. If every start overlaps a salt month, the app gives a warning and links to the rotation cards.

The app uses today’s date to choose the next window, including across New Year. It shows “sow now” within the window up to day 1 of its final month. After that day it shows the next window. Where all months are safe, the harvest and water example uses a December start.

**Not used:** SMAP soil moisture (9 km pixels read flooded fields as open water), ECOSTRESS (too few cloud-free passes) and the Landsat soil-salinity maps of Wang et al. 2025 (they track water and greenness, not salt).

## The 6 provinces
<details><summary>Show how the 14 former provinces map to the 6, and what is computed for each</summary>

The app covers the south first: the 5 Mekong Delta provinces (Đồng Tháp, An Giang, Vĩnh Long, Cần Thơ, Cà Mau) and Tây Ninh, which since 1 July 2025 includes former Long An (National Assembly Resolution 202/2025/QH15; regions from Government Resolution 306/NQ-CP, 5 Oct 2025: Tây Ninh is in the Southeast, the others in the Mekong Delta). Until November 2026 it covered all 34 provinces; the rest were removed so the south can be refined first, and can be added back in `pipeline/sites.py` (the fetch scripts are unchanged). `sites.py` lists each province with the former provinces it now covers, one data point per former province (its former capital; 14 in all, plus the 5 Sóc Trăng area pins = 19 points). `build.py` writes them to `docs/data/provinces.json` with its own source list; `sites.json` (the 5 areas the main app shows) is unchanged.

The model runs at all 19 points:

- **Each of the 14 province points** (`places` in `provinces.json`) gets the same engine as a Sóc Trăng pin: its own ERA5 weather and FAO-56 water demand, its own CHIRPS rain, its own SoilGrids topsoil, MODIS greenness where `data/raw/ndvi_<id>.json` exists (the 6 provinces' own points), a rain cross-check (CHIRPS against IMERG) and the El Niño view.
- **Each of the 6 provinces** (`provinces`) is the month-by-month average of its points' climates, and the average topsoil of its points with soil.
- **Soil:** most points are town centres, where SoilGrids has no soil. `py pipeline/fetch_soil.py nearby` re-samples those at the nearest farmland pixel 3–15 km away, always inside the same former province (checked against the outlines), and the app says how far away it is. Today all 19 points have soil: 6 at the point itself, 13 re-sampled 3 km away.
- **Salinity:** a point has an estimate only if a KTTV station lies within 25 km (6 of the 14 points: Long An, Tiền Giang, Bến Tre, Trà Vinh, Sóc Trăng, Hậu Giang; see the salinity row of [Where each number comes from](#where-each-number-comes-from)), and the 6 province averages never do. Otherwise the level is `unknown`, labelled as such, and the salt line names the nearest station and its distance. Without salt months, weather & salt is not scored, salt-month profit losses are not counted, shrimp's brackish-water need is not checked and no safe sowing window is given; confidence drops one level.
- **Rotation cards:** at the 12 Mekong Delta former provinces and former Long An (Delta rice land with salt data, now in Tây Ninh; `sites.SCORED_PLACES`), because the rotations and every published profit figure come from the Delta. Former Tây Ninh (upland) and the province averages get the weather, rain and soil evidence and a line saying why they are not scored. Cards are listed in a fixed order, not ranked.
- **MODIS greenness** exists for the 6 provinces' own points (11 files with the 5 areas). It is used only where the pixel looks like a field: a pixel never greener than 0.5 NDVI is a town or water, so it is shown as such and not used (5 of the 6 today, because the points are former provincial capitals). It is not used for the province averages. Either way, no usable MODIS costs one confidence level.

**One map.** The planner's map (`app/map.js`) zooms out to the Mekong Delta and Tây Ninh, and `app/vn.js` draws them on it: zoom out past 9.25 (or tap **ĐBSCL** in the top bar, or open `index.html#vietnam`; the old `vietnam.html` address goes there) and the 6 provinces appear around the 5 Sóc Trăng areas, which keep their salt colours and taps; zoomed in, the planner looks as before, with only the province outlines added. Panning stops a little beyond the area. It shows Esri satellite imagery (offline tiles for the area, online beyond), the 6 provinces as white dashed outlines with the borders of their former provinces dotted inside, a see-through colour layer blended between the 14 points (yearly or monthly CHIRPS rain, or rain minus crop water need, or salt; typical or El Niño years; the salt layer blends between the KTTV stations, each month taking the station's own bulletin reading for that month (the highest 10-day reading, averaged over the typical or El Niño seasons that have one) and, in months no bulletin covers, its salt-month estimate; the "Mặn tháng này" tile uses the same monthly value, so map and tile agree; and is clipped to the former provinces whose point has an estimate; in El Niño its key notes that the 5 Sóc Trăng areas still show a typical year) and gold pins with name plates. Tapping a province opens its average and each former province's own numbers, chart, cards and sources. Outlines: `py pipeline/make_outlines.py` joins geoBoundaries' former provinces in `sites.PROVINCES` (OCHA ROAP + Government of Viet Nam 2020, CC BY 3.0 IGO, the same source as the district outlines) into the new ones, keeps shared borders identical after simplifying, and checks that every pin sits inside its own province.

Points: `pipeline/sites.py`; download: `py pipeline/fetch_era5.py` (skips points already saved this month; backs off on HTTP 429 and stops on the daily quota: run it again later), `py pipeline/fetch_enso.py`, `py pipeline/fetch_chirps.py` (and `py pipeline/fetch_gpm.py` for the cross-check), `py pipeline/fetch_soil.py` then `py pipeline/fetch_soil.py nearby`; outlines: `py pipeline/make_outlines.py`.

## How the scores are set
<details><summary>Show every formula, the score table and the published profit figures</summary>

Each rotation is rated on water, income, soil, pests and weather & salt from 1.0 to 5.0, where higher is better. The app omits income for mung bean. The match is the slider-weighted average of the available scores (equal weights if their sliders are all zero), with a 0.5-point deduction for an upland crop when labour or equipment is limited, and each card's "Why suggested" shows the numbers behind them.

| Score | Rubric |
|---|---|
| Water | Computed at each pin, not set by hand: the share of 3 rice crops' yearly canal water the rotation saves. Score = 1 + 4 × that share (0% = 1.0, 50% = 3.0, 100% = 5.0); no cut-offs. Dropping the autumn–winter crop saves little, because it grows in the rainy season; resting or growing an upland crop in the dry months saves much more. |
| Income | Computed at each pin: 3 + 2 × log2(P ÷ P3), kept between 1 and 5, so the same profit as 3 rice crops = 3.0, double = 5.0, half = 1.0. **P** = the rotation's yearly profit: for each crop in its calendar, the median of the published profit figures for that crop (table below; full figures only where there are at least 2) × (1 − loss share), added up. **P3** = the same for 3 rice crops at the same pin and view. **Loss:** in the typical view, shrimp loses 9.2% to disease (Nhân Dân 2021, Sóc Trăng rice–shrimp: damaged shrimp area 10%, then 8.4%). In the El Niño view the typical losses still apply, and ordinary rice and upland crops growing in the pin's salt months lose 10% of their estimated profit: the Delta's winter–spring harvest fell by 1.1 of 11.1 million tonnes in 2016 (General Statistics Office). Using the same 10% for the other rice crops and the upland crop, and 5% for ST rice (10% × the half count used for salt-tolerant rice), is **our team estimate**. Where brackish water is not normally available, shrimp counts 0. |
| Soil | Computed from each rotation's 12-month calendar: 1 + 4 × (0.45 × legume + 0.2 × cover + 0.2 × dry break + 0.15 × fewer rice months) − brackish, kept between 1 and 5, the same scale as Pests. **Legume** = min(legume months ÷ 2, 1) (mung bean; Rahman et al. 2014). **Cover crop** = min(rest months ÷ 2, 1), only for the salt-tolerant option, whose rest period may be a cover crop. **Dry break** = min((rest + upland months) ÷ 3, 1): months when the flooded soil drains and dries out between rice crops (shrimp months stay flooded and do not count). **Fewer rice months** = 1 − rice months ÷ 12. **Brackish** = 0.5 if the calendar has shrimp months, otherwise 0: salt from the brackish phase stays in the soil until rain flushes it (Ngoc et al. 2023, Heliyon, Bạc Liêu: "Putative exchange (exch.) Na+ hydrolysis occurs at the beginning of the rainy season when excess salts are leached."). No source gives these weights, the 3 dry months or the 0.5 as numbers, so they are **our team estimates** and cost one confidence level. The old formula (1 + legume + cover − brackish) could not go above 2.5 and gave 1.0 to most rotations; this one spreads them from 1.3 to 3.9. Same at every pin. |
| Pests | Computed at each pin: 1 + 4 × (1 − risk), kept between 1 and 5. Each calendar month has a pressure from 0 to 1. **Rice:** brown planthopper pressure at the month's mean ERA5 temperature ((daily maximum + daily minimum) ÷ 2), read off Horgan et al. 2020 (PLoS ONE 15(6): e0235506): 0 at 20 °C or below ("did not develop to adults at 15 and 20°C"), 1 from 25 to 30 °C ("egg-laying peaked at 30°C"; 0.87 of nymphs reached adults at 25–30 °C), 0 at 35 °C ("failed to develop beyond the fourth instar at 35°C"), linear in between. **Upland crop:** 1 in every month it is in the field (maize: fall armyworm; watermelon: melon thrips; mung bean: whitefly and pod borer), because no Mekong Delta source gives their timing. **Shrimp:** 0.5 + 0.5 × the share of the month's days at or above 35 °C (ERA5-Land); white spot disease recurs, so shrimp months never fall below 0.5. **Rest:** 0. **Risk** = (rice pressure × (1 − 0.5 × break) + the other crops' pressure) ÷ 12, with break = 0.5 × min(rest months ÷ 2, 1) + 0.5 × min(non-rice months ÷ 2, 1) (PhilRice 2018; Oka, JARQ 26(2)). Two constants are **our team estimates** (no source gives a number): the 0.5 shrimp floor and the 0.5 break credit. The 35 °C heat rule is the one used in Weather & salt (Kiên Giang report). Monthly mean temperatures at the pins are 25.8–30.1 °C in a typical year, so rice pressure is flat here (0.98 to 1; April at Mỹ Xuyên and Ngã Năm is 30.1 °C, 0.98); in an El Niño year they are 26.3–31.6 °C and April at those two pins (31.6 °C) falls to 0.68. The score separates rotations by their calendar, not by place. There is no cold-night rule: no source gives a drop or range that daily gridded data can count (`research/cold-snap-shrimp.md`), and the old 4.2 °C rule, a team estimate, never fired on the gridded data either. Shrimp months count the 0.5 floor plus hot days only. |
| Weather & salt | Computed at each pin: 0.6 × salt + 0.2 × heat + 0.2 × canal water (weights are our team's choice). Each part is 1 + 4 × (1 − risk), so no risk = 5.0 and full risk = 1.0. **Salt:** the share of this pin's salt months with a crop in the field (salt-tolerant rice counts half, our estimate; shrimp and rest count 0), times the station's average dry-season peak ÷ 15‰ (at most 1; 4 ÷ 15 at Ngã Năm, where sluices keep fields fresh). **Heat:** ERA5-Land days at or above 35 °C in months with rice or an upland crop, ÷ 30 days (at most 1). **Canal water:** canal water the rotation needs in the salt months ÷ what 3 rice crops need then (at most 1). The El Niño view uses the El Niño seasons' station readings, heat and rain. Where there is no salinity estimate (the 34 province averages and 55 of the 63 points), weather & salt is not scored: its salt and canal parts need the salt months. |

We found no usable 2020–2026 profit figure for mung bean in the Delta, so its income is not scored: a Nov 2026 search found only two, both in one Báo Cà Mau article (3 Apr 2020, baocamau.vn/nong-dan-that-mua-dau-xanh-a3129.html), one from before 2020 and one a drought-year profit worked out as revenue minus cost with the công size not stated. For shrimp and rice in rice–shrimp, the table holds the figures that split the crops; figures that cover the whole system are listed below instead.

**Cross-checks** (not used in the score): 3 rice crops ≈ 95.4 M ₫/ha a year here, against 30.3 M per crop × 3 = 90.8 M on 60 Hậu Giang farms in 2024 (Tất Anh Thư et al. 2025, Cần Thơ Univ. journal, Table 7). 2 rice crops ≈ 65.4 M, against 63.0 M a year from one Cần Thơ farmer's 2 crops (Tạp chí Công Thương, 2022). Rice–shrimp ≈ 76.2 M a year, against whole-system figures of 40–60 M (Bạc Liêu, 2022, [Viet Nam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/society/1154533/mekong-delta-to-expand-shrimp-%E2%80%93-rice-farming-model.html)), 70–100 M (Thạnh Phú, Bến Tre, 2023, district agriculture office, [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/hieu-qua-kep-cua-mo-hinh-lua-tom-post755015.html)), 1.3× rice on the same land (Sóc Trăng, 2025, [Báo Sóc Trăng](https://baosoctrang.org.vn/nong-nghiep/202501/buoc-tien-cua-tinh-soc-trang-trong-qua-trinh-co-cau-lai-nganh-nong-nghiep-c3d2e62/)) and about 4× rice alone (WWF study, unpublished, The Fish Site 2024). These whole-system figures stay out of the crop table so that shrimp and rice are not counted twice.

**Site rules** (in `pipeline/build.py`, each covered by a test):
1. **Brackish water.** Where salinity is low, rice–shrimp counts no shrimp profit (income 1.0), loses 1 confidence level and is ranked last as "not usually possible here".
2. **Field calendar (NASA MODIS and HLS).** Where greenness shows fields usually bare (more than half the years) in at least one month of February–April, in MODIS (250 m) or in HLS (30 m), the upland and salt-tolerant options gain 1 confidence level, because the gap they need already exists. This is our team's rule.

## How we checked it
<details><summary>Show all checks and their numbers</summary>

- **Pins sit on rice.** Each pin must look like rice in both NASA greenness records. MODIS (250 m): 2–3 green peaks a year with bare or flooded dips (`appeears.py find`). HLS (30 m), pooled over the pin's 9 points: 90th-percentile NDVI of at least 0.7, 10th percentile below 0.35, and water flagged on at most 10% of clear dates (`appeears.py find-hls`; `test_build.py` fails a pin that does not pass). We added the 30 m check in November 2026, after sharper imagery showed the Vĩnh Châu pin among shrimp ponds and the Ngã Năm pin on garden land at the edge of town: their 250 m MODIS pixels had mixed in greener land and passed. Vĩnh Châu moved 8 km north, from 9.365, 105.96 to 9.435, 105.98, because there was no rice within 6 km of the old spot, only ponds and tree-lined dikes; it is now labelled "rice, inland". Ngã Năm moved 1.3 km south, from 9.56, 105.60 to 9.548, 105.6. Mỹ Xuyên had been moved 3.3 km onto rice in the earlier MODIS check. On the last 10 seasons (Dec 2014 – Nov 2024) MODIS shows 1.9–2.4 crops a year at the five pins, yearly peak greenness 0.87–0.92 and dips to 0.10–0.21; in 2000–2014 the same pixels showed 1.9–2.5 crops a year. After the move, Vĩnh Châu's water needs fell 1–9% and its salt-tolerant rice card rose from low to medium confidence (its fields are now bare in March–April). No ranking changed, and Ngã Năm's cards did not change.
- **A 30 m check of the bare months (NASA HLS; no score uses it).** We read NASA HLS greenness (Landsat 8/9 and Sentinel-2, 30 m; `appeears.py hls`) at 9 points 30 m apart around each pin, Dec 2014 – Nov 2024. Only cloud-free dates count (Fmask), and each date's value is the median of its clear points. A month is bare when its mean NDVI is below 0.3 in more than half of the years with a clear date. It is unknown when fewer than 5 of the 10 years have one. Clear dates are scarce: about 1–3 a month in the dry season, under 1 in July–October.

  | Pin | MODIS bare months (used) | HLS bare months | HLS unknown |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Vĩnh Châu | Mar–Jun, Oct | Mar–Jun, Oct | none |
  | Trần Đề | Mar–May, Oct | Mar–May | none |
  | Mỹ Xuyên | Apr | Mar–Jun, Oct | Jul |
  | Long Phú | May | May, Sep | none |
  | Ngã Năm | Sep–Nov | Apr, Sep–Nov | none |

  For February–April, the months the field-calendar rule reads, the two lead to the same result at four pins. At Ngã Năm, HLS counts April as bare and MODIS does not. Since November 2026 a month bare in either record counts for the rule, so Ngã Năm's salt-tolerant card rose from medium to high confidence (its upland card stays at medium, capped by thin income figures). The cards name HLS when the bare month comes only from it. The first run of this check, with the old Vĩnh Châu and Ngã Năm pins, disagreed at Vĩnh Châu; that is how we found the pins were not on rice (see "Pins sit on rice"). Per-pin numbers: `hls_check` in `docs/data/sites.json`.
- **Satellite rain finds the known droughts.** Ranking dry seasons by water shortfall (Dec–Apr, ET₀ minus all rain, at Trần Đề): an earlier check over 1984–2024 with NASA POWER rain and Penman–Monteith ET₀ (no longer in the repository) put 1997–98, 1987–88 and 1986–87 driest, all El Niño winters in NOAA's index; over Dec 2000 – Nov 2025 with CHIRPS rain, 3 of the 5 driest are El Niño seasons by RONI (2002–03, 2023–24 and 2004–05; the driest, 2019–20, and the second, 2001–02, are not El Niño by RONI; 2009–10 is sixth and 2015–16 seventh), with ERA5 ET₀. 2019–20 (first here) and 2015–16 are the years Vietnamese reports describe as the worst recent droughts and salt intrusion.
- **Two rain sources move together.** CHIRPS rain (~5 km, used by the app) and NASA GPM IMERG rain (~10 km) correlate at 0.93–0.94 month to month at the 5 pins over the 298 months Dec 2000 – Sep 2025; CHIRPS's yearly totals are within −6% to +1% of IMERG's, and its Dec–Apr totals are 5–14% lower (numbers below). Both read high against the one gauge we could use (Cà Mau), IMERG more so.
- **Weather source: a station test.** ERA5-based sources match three Mekong Delta stations (Cần Thơ, Cà Mau, Rạch Giá; NOAA GSOD, 2011–2025) better than NASA POWER (numbers below); the app therefore uses ERA5 for temperature and ET₀ and ERA5-Land for heat days.
- **The water method reproduces FAO's example.** `test_build.py` recomputes FAO Manual 3's worked rice example (April: ET crop 198 mm + seepage 150 + water layer 100 − effective rain 135 = 313 mm) with the app's own function.
- **Every claim has a link.** Each source in `pipeline/rotations.json`, `salinity.json`, `profits.json` and `resources.json` carries a URL and a note on what it supports. The app labels a source "(not yet checked against the source)" until the specific claim has been found in it; the source's note then gives a short quote and where it appears, and `verified` is set to `true`.
- **Water, heat and salinity in the scores.** Water and weather & salt are straight lines in the share of water saved, the station reading, hot days and canal water, so a small change in the data moves a score a little instead of flipping it. The salinity level (high from 15‰, medium from 4‰) labels the area in its summary; a low level triggers the brackish-water rule for shrimp. A high level adds an explanation on its card. Medium or high salinity also triggers the upland warning when crop and salt months overlap.
- **Confidence comes from the evidence, not from a hand-set label.** Each card starts at High and drops one level for each of: (1) income resting on fallback figures (fewer than 2 qualifying published figures for a crop), on figures that differ by more than 2× between lowest and highest, or on no figure at all (mung bean); (2) no NASA MODIS NDVI loaded for the pin, or a MODIS pixel never greener than 0.5 NDVI (a town or water, not a field; province points only); (3) a salinity station with fewer than 5 recent dry seasons or no reading after 2022; (4) rice–shrimp where brackish water is not normally available; (5) team estimates used by that card (salt-tolerant rice counted as half a rice month, salt left by the shrimp phase, the El Niño crop-loss share where it applies, the 0.5 break credit for pests, the soil-score weights and dry-break months, the pest timing for upland crops, the shrimp disease floor), together one level. Fields usually bare in February–April (in MODIS or HLS) raise the upland and salt-tolerant options one level (the field-calendar rule under "How the scores are set"). Drop (1) also caps the card at Medium, so this bonus cannot cancel thin income evidence. The reasons are listed on the card under its risks.
- **Salinity readings checked two ways.** (Sóc Trăng pins.) Our typing of the station table reproduces the paper's own count, minimum, maximum and mean for all 8 stations. The national forecast centre's 10-day bulletins (NCHMF; the 20 found online for 2024–2026 are in `data/raw/salinity_bulletins.json`; the 30 used for the province points are in `data/raw/salinity_kttv_vietnam.json`) are an independent record: their highest 2024 and 2025 readings equal the table's at 9 of 10 station-years in the 20-bulletin file. The 10th, Thạnh Phú in 2025, peaked in May, after the last bulletin in that file; the 10 May 2025 bulletin found later (in the 30-bulletin file) has Thạnh Phú at 4.2‰, the table's 2025 peak, so all 10 now agree.
- **KTTV 10-day salt forecast.** The forecast table (Bảng 2, "Smax" per station, g/l read as ‰) of the newest national bulletin is parsed by `fetch_salinity_vietnam.parse_forecast` into `data/raw/salinity_kttv_vietnam.json` (`forecast`), with the issue date, period and the bulletin's disaster-risk level. A layout it does not know fails the fetch and keeps the last good forecast. Each pin and province point takes its nearest KTTV station's value within 25 km (`salinity.forecast_at`). The app labels it a KTTV forecast with the issue date and hides it 14 days after its period ends (`docs/app/ahead.js`); the 31 May 2026 bulletin, the last of the season, is carried but hidden from mid-June 2026.
- **3-month rain outlook (wetter / drier / near normal).** `pipeline/fetch_seasonal.py` asks the Open-Meteo Seasonal API (`seasonal-api.open-meteo.com/v1/seasonal`, model `ecmwf_seas5`, ensemble mean, 36 km, not bias-corrected) for monthly `precipitation_mean` and `precipitation_anomaly` at each of the 5 Sóc Trăng pins and writes `data/raw/seasonal.json` (exit 1 and no file if any pin fails). The API gives the anomaly against its own hindcast climatology, so `anom_pct = anomaly / (mean - anomaly) x 100`, with no outside climatology. The label is a team choice, not an ECMWF or WMO rule: beyond +20% wetter, below -20% drier, else near normal. The API does not state the run date, so the issue date is the 1st of the first month returned (SEAS5 runs start on the 1st and are published about the 5th). Pins only; province points get none. Shown as words, never mm; the app hides it 45 days after the issue date or when its months are over (`docs/app/ahead.js`). Open-Meteo data are CC BY 4.0, free for non-commercial use (open-meteo.com/en/terms, checked 24 Nov 2026), hence the credit line. Skill is limited: mostly the first month and ENSO-driven years (see `research/research_notes/Mekong app data source alternatives/new_layers.md`).
- **Rice price line (Bạc Liêu wholesale, 5% broken).** `pipeline/fetch_prices.py` lists FAO FPMA's Viet Nam series (`FpmaSerieDomestic/?iso3_country_code=VNM`), picks the one whose market is Bạc Liêu (FPMA writes "Bac Lieu") and whose commodity is "Rice (5% broken)" (exactly one, else exit 1), reads its `FpmaSeriePrice/<uuid>` datapoints and keeps FPMA's monthly averages in VND/kg in `data/raw/rice_price.json` (with the uuid, 756c8234-c859-404c-9752-20bd51745801 on 24 Nov 2026; 98 months, Oct 2018 to Nov 2026). FPMA republishes the Vietnam Food Association's weekly prices (vietfood.org.vn/thi-truong/gia-noi-dia/). `build.rice_price` takes the median of every month from 2020-01 to 2026-12 that the series has (83 months on 24 Nov 2026: 10,550 ₫/kg) and compares the latest month with it (Nov 2026: 12,000 ₫/kg, +13.7%). The span 2020-26 is a team choice. The profit figures are fixed published medians and are not rescaled by this price; the line says so. The app hides the line when the latest month is more than 3 months old (team choice; `Ahead.price`, `docs/app/ahead.js`). Labels: FPMA's series metadata says `price_type` WHOLESALE but `market_type` "Retail"; we keep "wholesale", as the series name does. It is a milled-rice price, not a paddy (lúa) or farm-gate price. Shown on the dashboard money tile and card, under each planner card's comparison, and in the step 4 money block. FPMA answers 403 to Python's default User-Agent, so the fetcher sends another.
- **El Niño seasons are saltier.** Over the dry seasons 1999–2026, the average highest reading was higher in El Niño seasons than in other seasons at 4 of the 5 pins' stations (Thạnh Phú 8.5‰ vs 12.5‰, Long Phú 16.1‰ vs 18.7‰, Ngã Năm 9.7‰ vs 11.5‰, Trần Đề 24.5‰ vs 27.0‰; not Khánh Hòa, whose only El Niño seasons are 2016 and 2019). Over 2001–2024 the same holds at all 8 stations.
- **Self-checks.** `python pipeline/test_build.py` runs 69 checks on the calculations and on the data the app reads.

## Using it anywhere in the Delta
<details><summary>Show what each input needs for a new area, and the steps</summary>

The five pins are a demonstration, not a limit of the method. Every NASA dataset the app uses covers the whole Mekong Delta, so most of the work for a new pin is running the same scripts. The local parts are salinity and the crop calendars.

| Input | Covers | For a new pin |
|---|---|---|

| SoilGrids topsoil (not NASA) | The whole globe, 250 m | Automatic: `fetch_soil.py` |

3. Run `fetch_era5.py`, `fetch_enso.py`, `fetch_chirps.py`, `fetch_gpm.py`, `fetch_soil.py`, `appeears.py ndvi` and `appeears.py find <pin_id> --apply` (see **For teammates: updating the data**).

## Limitations in full
<details><summary>Show the full list</summary>

- Five preset pins, not any field. Weather describes a ~10–50 km area and greenness one 250 m pixel (see the data table).
- The typical and El Niño years' weather and rain are averages of Dec 2000 to the newest whole Dec–Nov season (Nov 2025 now; the end rolls forward as CHIRPS and ERA5 grow, `build.PERIOD`), not forecasts (salinity uses the dry seasons 1999–2026; see the data table). The 8 El Niño seasons range from weak (RONI 0.5) to very strong (2.1). NOAA's forecast only chooses which view the app opens on; a very strong El Niño such as the one forecast for 2026–27 may be harsher than the 8-season average.
- Salinity comes from river and canal stations, not from fields, and the station is sometimes several kilometres away. Typical-year averages rest on 8–19 dry seasons (Khánh Hòa 8, Ngã Năm 9, Long Phú 17, Thạnh Phú 18, Trần Đề 19) and El Niño averages on 2–8 (Khánh Hòa 2, Ngã Năm 3, Long Phú and Thạnh Phú 7, Trần Đề 8), and the Khánh Hòa station (Vĩnh Châu) has no season peak after 2022 (2026 has one mid-April reading, counted only as a lower bound). Dù Tho (the old Tham Đôn station, upstream on the same Mỹ Thanh river) was checked as a replacement and not used: only 2025 is online in full (daily bulletins in Báo Sóc Trăng, peak 15.8‰ on 9 March), other years have scattered values that are not peaks, and no day reports both it and Khánh Hòa. Its 2017–2023 monthly bulletins were on sotnmt.soctrang.gov.vn and sonnmt.soctrang.gov.vn, which could not be reached from outside Vietnam in November 2026. For the other provinces, station positions are approximate OpenStreetMap points (three possibly 10 km or more off), the bulletins found cover only part of the dry seasons (January and February of 2024 and 2026 are not online), so a partial season may miss an earlier peak, and the El Niño view rests on the single season 2024. Only 6 of the 14 province points have an estimate.
- The rotation calendars are typical Sóc Trăng timing from provincial reports; real sowing dates move by weeks from year to year and field to field. Seepage uses one FAO value for every pin.
- ERA5 is 0.25° (~28 km): Vĩnh Châu, Trần Đề, Mỹ Xuyên and Long Phú sit in one cell (9.5°N, 106.0°E) and get identical temperature and ET₀ (typical-year Dec–Apr ET₀ 657 mm, whole year 1,440 mm); Ngã Năm is in the next cell (649 mm, 1,393 mm). Before, with NASA POWER, the Dec–Apr ET₀ was 683 / 706 / 716 / 706 / 716 mm at Vĩnh Châu / Trần Đề / Mỹ Xuyên / Long Phú / Ngã Năm, so it fell by 4–10%, less than the 15–20% the station test suggested for its stations. ERA5-Land (0.1°) gives the heat days. Typical-year heat days per year: Vĩnh Châu 0.1, Trần Đề 0.2, Mỹ Xuyên 0.4, Long Phú 0.2, Ngã Năm 3.0 (El Niño view 0.6, 0.6, 5.1, 1.8, 15.7), against 0–28 with POWER. The heat part of the weather & salt score is therefore small: ERA5-Land matches the stations far better than POWER, which over-counted heat days 2.4 to 24 times (station test above). The planner's 7-day weather strip reads Open-Meteo's forecast API live (model values, past 7 days).
- Heat counts days at or above 35 °C on a ~10 km ERA5-Land grid (a model, not a station: at three stations it counted about as many days as the station at two of them and about 2.7 times fewer at the airport at Cần Thơ); the 30-day reference is our choice. Rice is most hurt by heat at flowering (Yoshida 1981, IRRI), but the score counts every month with a crop, because sowing dates vary.
- Pests: rice pressure comes from a lab curve (Horgan et al. 2020, Philippine colony), not from Delta field counts, and monthly means at the pins sit in its 25–30 °C plateau, so it is flat across months and places. The two ERA5 cells that hold the five pins give pins in one cell the same weather-driven pressure. Upland and shrimp pest pressures follow the calendar with team-estimate constants; real outbreaks follow weather, variety and local control.
- Soil values are a 250 m model. Acid-sulfate soils common in the Delta vary from field to field.
- Scores are our rules from published sources, not calibrated against farm yields or incomes, and the app has no market prices. Profits are medians of published figures from other places and years (see **Published profit figures**), not from your farm; the El Niño loss shares rest on one Delta-wide harvest drop (2016) and our estimates.
- Seed, fertiliser and steps in the plan come from extension guides (2015–2025) checked against each source. Mung bean and maize figures are national norms and their steps come from guides for central and northern Vietnam (we found no Mekong guide with numbers), so the app labels them. Rice fertiliser is for alluvial soil; acid-sulfate soils need different rates.
- Upland crops: where their months meet the salt months at a medium- or high-salinity pin, the card warns that river water is far saltier than FAO lists for maize (watermelon and mung bean have no FAO number); fields with stored fresh water may do better. Which crops are grown in each area rests on a few news reports, and we found none for Vĩnh Châu.
- Fields are saved only in the phone's browser (no account): clearing site data, a private window or another phone starts again from one field. The 12-month plan reuses the rotation's typical calendar, not this year's sowing dates.

</details>
